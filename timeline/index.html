Timeline of the Roadless Rule: A Regulatory Rollercoaster (1998–2026)

Source: Adapted in part from Earthjustice, “Timeline of the Roadless Rule.” Available at: https://earthjustice.org/feature/timeline-of-the-roadless-rule

Steps to delay, block, narrow, exempt or roll back the ruleSteps to propose, impose, uphold or reinstate the rule
  1. 1998

    January 1998

    Moratorium proposed

    The U.S. Forest Service Chief announces plans to develop a transportation policy for the National Forest System. He also proposes an 18-month road building moratorium on 130 National Forests while the policy is developed.

    Source: 63 Fed. Reg. 4,350–4,351 (Jan. 28, 1998); 63 Fed. Reg. 4,351–4,354 (Jan. 28, 1998)

  2. 1999

    March 1, 1999

    Moratorium takes effect

    A temporary roadbuilding moratorium takes effect.

    Source: 64 Fed. Reg. 7,290–7,305 (Feb. 12, 1999) (effective Mar. 1, 1999)

  3. 2001

    January 2001

    Roadless Rule published

    The USDA publishes the Roadless Area Conservation Rule, generally prohibiting road construction, road reconstruction, and timber harvesting on 58.5 million acres of undeveloped national forest lands, subject to exceptions.

    Source: 66 Fed. Reg. 3,244, 3,245, 3,272–3,273 (Jan. 12, 2001)

  4. February 5, 2001

    Rule delayed

    In accordance with President Bush's Regulatory Review Plan, the Roadless Rule is delayed for an additional 60 calendar days.

    Source: 66 Fed. Reg. 8,899 (Feb. 5, 2001)

  5. May 10, 2001

    Idaho court blocks the rule

    Idaho Federal Judge Edward Lodge issues a preliminary injunction barring the Roadless Rule from taking effect.

    Source: Kootenai Tribe of Idaho v. Veneman, No. CV01-10-N-EJL, 2001 WL 1141275, at *2 (D. Idaho May 10, 2001)

  6. 2002

    December 12, 2002

    Ninth Circuit reinstates the rule

    The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals reverses Judge Lodge’s preliminary injunction, reinstating the Roadless Rule.

    Source: Kootenai Tribe of Idaho v. Veneman, 313 F.3d 1094, 1126 (9th Cir. 2002)

  7. 2003

    June 9, 2003

    Alaska settlement

    The Bush Administration announces it will settle a lawsuit brought by the State of Alaska by agreeing to propose a temporary exemption for the Tongass National Forest. Separately, it announces it will consider whether the Tongass and Chugach National Forests should be exempted from the Roadless Rule.

    Source: U.S. Dep't of Agric., Release No. 0200.03 (June 9, 2003); see also 68 Fed. Reg. 75,136, 75,136–75,137 (Dec. 30, 2003)

  8. July 14, 2003

    Wyoming court strikes the rule

    Wyoming Federal Court Judge Clarence Brimmer finds the Roadless Rule illegal and issues an injunction barring it from taking effect.

    Source: Wyoming v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 277 F. Supp. 2d 1197, 1239 (D. Wyo. 2003)

  9. December 30, 2003

    Tongass temporarily exempted

    USDA publishes a final rule temporarily exempting the Tongass National Forest from the Roadless Rule.

    Source: 68 Fed. Reg. 75,136–75,146 (Dec. 30, 2003)

  10. 2004

    January 29, 2004

    Tongass exemption takes effect

    The Tongass exemption takes effect.

    Source: 68 Fed. Reg. 75,136 (Dec. 30, 2003) (effective Jan. 29, 2004)

  11. 2005

    May 5, 2005

    State Petitions Rule

    The Bush Administration announces the State Petitions Rule as a substitute for the Roadless Rule, allowing governors to petition for state-specific management requirements.

    Source: 70 Fed. Reg. 25,654–25,662 (May 13, 2005) (signed May 5, 2005)

  12. 2006

    June 21, 2006

    Eastern state petitions approved

    The Bush Administration approves petitions from the governors of three Eastern states—Virginia, North Carolina, and South Carolina—concerning state-specific roadless protections for roughly 555,000 acres of forested land within their borders.

    Source: Pamela Baldwin & Ross Gorte, Cong. Rsch. Serv., RL30647, The National Forest System Roadless Areas Initiative 31 & nn.122–123 (updated Sept. 7, 2006)

  13. September 20, 2006

    Court reinstates the Roadless Rule

    Federal District Court Judge Elizabeth Laporte in San Francisco orders reinstatement of the Clinton-era Roadless Rule for almost 50 million acres of national forests and grasslands, generally restricting road construction, road reconstruction, and timber harvesting, subject to exceptions.

    Source: California ex rel. Lockyer v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., No. C05-03508 EDL (N.D. Cal. Sept. 20, 2006), amended, 459 F. Supp. 2d 874, 919 (N.D. Cal. Oct. 11, 2006)

  14. 2007

    February 6, 2007

    Final injunction issued

    Judge Laporte issues a final injunction, clarifying its application to certain post-May 2005 oil-and-gas authorizations.

    Source: California ex rel. Lockyer v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., Nos. C05-03508 EDL & C05-04038 EDL, Final Injunction Order, Dkt. 235, at 2–3 (N.D. Cal. Feb. 6, 2007)

  15. 2008

    August 12, 2008

    Wyoming court strikes the rule again

    Wyoming Federal District Court Judge Brimmer issues a second decision, very similar to his 2003 decision, invalidating and enjoining the Roadless Rule nationwide.

    Source: Wyoming v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 570 F. Supp. 2d 1309, 1354–1355 (D. Wyo. 2008)

  16. October 16, 2008

    Idaho Roadless Rule adopted

    The Forest Service adopts the Idaho Roadless Rule.

    Source: 73 Fed. Reg. 61,456–61,496 (Oct. 16, 2008)

  17. December 2, 2008

    Injunction narrowed

    Judge Laporte partially stays her injunction, limiting geographic scope to the Ninth Circuit and New Mexico.

    Source: California ex rel. Lockyer v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., Nos. C05-03508 EDL & C05-04038 EDL, Order Partially Staying Injunctive Relief, Dkt. 283 (N.D. Cal. Dec. 2, 2008)

  18. December 5, 2008

    Colorado rule postponed

    Colorado and Administration officials agree to postpone action on the proposed Colorado Roadless Rule until spring 2009.

    Source: Colorado Secures Delay in Implementation of Roadless Management Plan, Am. Whitewater (Dec. 5, 2008)

  19. 2009

    August 5, 2009

    Ninth Circuit upholds the rule

    The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals upholds Roadless Rule restrictions affecting over 40 million acres of national forests and grasslands, generally limiting road construction, road reconstruction, and timber harvesting.

    Source: California ex rel. Lockyer v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 575 F.3d 999, 1021 (9th Cir. 2009)

  20. 2010

    May 28, 2010

    Secretary review extended

    The Obama Administration extends for another year a requirement that certain road construction, road reconstruction, and timber cutting or removal decisions in inventoried roadless areas be reserved to the Secretary of Agriculture.

    Source: U.S. Dep't of Agric., Secretary's Memorandum 1042-155 (May 28, 2010), cited in Kristina Alexander & Ross W. Gorte, Cong. Rsch. Serv., RL30647, National Forest System (NFS) Roadless Area Initiatives 18 n.97 (Apr. 18, 2011)

  21. 2011

    January 29, 2011

    Idaho rule upheld

    Idaho District Court upholds the Idaho Roadless Rule.

    Source: Jayne v. Rey, 780 F. Supp. 2d 1099 (D. Idaho 2011)

  22. March 4, 2011

    Tongass exemption vacated

    Alaska District Court vacates the 2003 Tongass exemption and reinstates the Roadless Rule on the Tongass.

    Source: Organized Village of Kake v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 776 F. Supp. 2d 960, 976–977 (D. Alaska 2011)

  23. October 21, 2011

    Tenth Circuit upholds the rule

    The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals reverses the Wyoming District Court and directs that the prior injunction be vacated.

    Source: Wyoming v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 661 F.3d 1209, 1272 (10th Cir. 2011)

  24. 2012

    July 3, 2012

    Colorado Roadless Rule adopted

    The Forest Service adopts the Colorado Roadless Rule.

    Source: U.S. Forest Serv., Colorado Roadless Rule

  25. 2014

    March 26, 2014

    Tongass exemption revived

    A divided panel of the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals reverses the 2011 Alaska District Court decision by a 2–1 split vote, reviving the Tongass exemption.

    Source: Organized Village of Kake v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 746 F.3d 970 (9th Cir. 2014)

  26. August 29, 2014

    Rehearing en banc granted

    The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit announces it will re-hear the case challenging the Tongass exemption. The court grants the rehearing en banc, rendering the earlier opinion ineffective.

    Source: Organized Village of Kake v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 765 F.3d 1117 (9th Cir. 2014)

  27. 2015

    July 29, 2015

    Tongass exemption struck down

    The Ninth Circuit's 11-judge en banc panel rules that the exemption of the Tongass from the Roadless Rule was invalid under the Administrative Procedure Act.

    Source: Organized Village of Kake v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., 795 F.3d 956, 966–970 (9th Cir. 2015) (en banc)

  28. 2018

    August 2, 2018

    Alaska rulemaking begins

    Alaska and the Forest Service enter an agreement as cooperating agencies for an Alaska roadless rulemaking after Alaska petitioned for a Tongass exemption.

    Source: Memorandum of Understanding Between the State of Alaska and USDA Forest Service (Aug. 2, 2018)

  29. 2020

    October 29, 2020

    Tongass exemption finalized

    The Trump Administration publishes a final rule exempting the Tongass National Forest from the Roadless Rule.

    Source: 85 Fed. Reg. 68,688–68,703 (Oct. 29, 2020)

  30. 2023

    January 27, 2023

    Tongass protections restored

    The U.S. Forest Service formally reinstates the 2001 Roadless Rule management regime across the Tongass National Forest, reimposing restrictions on road construction, road reconstruction, and timber harvesting, subject to exceptions, across about 9.37 million acres of land.

    Source: 88 Fed. Reg. 5,252–5,272 (Jan. 27, 2023)

  31. September 8, 2023

    Utilities sue USDA

    Represented by Pacific Legal Foundation, Inside Passage Electric Cooperative and Alaska Power Association file suit against USDA after the agency’s January 2023 decision reinstating the 2001 Roadless Rule on the Tongass National Forest. The USDA lacked statutory authority to reimpose the rule, and the restrictions impede hydroelectric, geothermal, and other infrastructure projects that require road access.

    Source: Complaint, Inside Passage Elec. Coop. v. U.S. Dep't of Agric., No. 3:23-cv-00204-SLG (D. Alaska Sept. 8, 2023)

  32. 2025

    January 20, 2025

    Executive order on the Tongass

    On his first day in office, President Trump signs an executive order directing the Secretary of Agriculture to reinstate the 2020 Alaska Roadless Rule’s exemption for the Tongass.

    Source: Exec. Order No. 14,153, § 3(c), 90 Fed. Reg. 8,347, 8,350 (Jan. 29, 2025) (signed Jan. 20, 2025)

  33. June 23, 2025

    Rescission announced

    Agriculture Secretary Rollins announces USDA’s intention to rescind the 2001 Roadless Rule, and USDA begins the formal rulemaking process.

    Source: U.S. Dep't of Agric., Secretary Rollins Rescinds Roadless Rule, Eliminating Impediment to Responsible Forest Management (June 23, 2025); see also 90 Fed. Reg. 42,179 (Aug. 29, 2025)

  34. 2026

    August 20, 2026

    Rescission proposed

    The U.S. Forest Service publishes a proposed rule and environmental review for rescinding the Roadless Rule.

    Source: 91 Fed. Reg. 53,827–53,832 (Aug. 20, 2026); see also 91 Fed. Reg. 57,841 (Sept. 11, 2026) (extending comment period)
